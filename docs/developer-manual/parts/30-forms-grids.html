Records

Sidebar: Developer › Records

A table holds the records people save: purchase requests, expense claims, headcount changes, deals. This screen defines each table's fields. Records are entered, searched and reviewed through a Records widget on a dashboard; posting record values into planning cells is configured under Integrations › Records.

Status and workflows

  • No status field, no status. A table without a status field gives its records no status: a record counts in the metrics its integrations post into as soon as it is saved, and saving it fires the table's submitted trigger.
  • A status field means approval. With one, a new record starts as a draft. Its creator may move it between draft and submitted; an administrator may set any status. An integration posts a record only in the statuses it names (approved by default). Records saved before the field was added keep no status and keep counting.
  • The workflow moves it. A workflow whose subject is the table (Single saved record in a table or Selected records in a table, under the workflow's Properties) and which has an approval step drives the status of the record its run is about — the record a form_submit trigger names: submitted while the run is open, approved or rejected by its last decision, a draft again if the run is cancelled. The record is posted again as it moves, as the approver. A test run moves nothing.
  • Adding or removing the field re-posts the table. Removing a table's status field makes every record count, so its records are posted again; the Save message says so.

Linked records

A table whose records are the members of a dimension can be linked to from any other table: a dimension field on that dimension names one of its records. A CRM's Accounts, Contacts and Deals tables keep the Account, Contact and Deal dimensions; a deal names its account and primary contact, a task its deal.

  • Create it from the form. In a record's form, a field naming such a dimension has New beside its picker for someone who may add records to the other table: that table's form opens in place, starts with the values both tables share (a new contact made from a deal already names the deal's account), and the new record is picked once saved.
  • What deleting it does. Each linking field has a rule (its Options: When that record is deleted): refuse while this record names it (the default — an account cannot be deleted while contacts or deals name it; the message says how many, in which table), delete this record with it (deleting a deal deletes its tasks, notes and document links, and so on down), or empty this field. The confirmation says what goes with the record before anything is deleted, and it all happens at once or not at all. A member a formula names cannot be deleted either.
  • A record's page. Clicking a row of a Records widget or a card of a Board opens the record as a page: its fields on the left (Edit to change them, when the person may), and on the right the records of other tables naming it — an account's contacts and deals, a contact's deals (as Primary Contact) — each table with how many there are, its first five fields as columns (in the table's field order, without the field naming this record: Move up in the table's editor brings a field into them) and Add, then the record's history. A related record, or a field naming a member a table keeps (a deal's account), opens in the same window; Back returns. The pencil opens the record's form as a dialog over the list.
  • Access follows belonging. Access rules on the dimension's members restrict who sees which records: a member hidden from a person hides that record and every record that belongs to it — through a field whose delete rule is refuse or delete with it, directly or through a chain (an account's contacts and deals, and those deals' tasks) — from their lists, searches, totals, exports, boards and pickers. A field whose rule is empty this field is a reference, not a belonging: a deal stays visible when its primary contact has moved to an account hidden from the person. Grids and charts follow the access rules as for any dimension.
  • Moves stay traceable. With Keep history on a linking field, a record moved to another (a contact who changed jobs) keeps the move in its history — from which record to which, who moved it and when — and the records naming it stay linked to it.
  • Revisions and packages. A revision copy keeps which dimension each table keeps, and a model package carries it; a package without data leaves the kept dimension empty, as its records are its members. Neither a revision copy nor a package carries the records' field history: it starts again in the new revision or the imported model, whose records are the importer's. Both rebuild what their tables' records post, so the grid totals stay whole when the next record is saved there.
  • Limits. Access rules hide or restrict the members they name: a person restricted from some accounts sees an account added later until it is restricted too. Grids and charts filter only the dimension an access rule names: a grid over the Contact dimension still lists the contacts of an account hidden from the reader. A table mapping posts a record only when every dimension it maps has a value — give mapped fields a default (an Unassigned member) or make them required.

Rules and behaviour

  • Tables are per revision. The list and the create action use the selected revision. Editing never moves a table to another revision.
  • Same name overwrites. Creating a table whose technical name already exists in the revision silently replaces that table's label and fields. Pick a new name for a new table.
  • Delete cascades. Deleting a table removes every dashboard widget that shows it and every record ever saved in it.
  • Records live on dashboards. Users and business admins work on records only in a dashboard Records widget (or a Board, see Dashboards): search, filters and a sort by any column (they cover every record the person may see, not one page), the pencil to edit a record (in a dialog over the list), the status list, delete, and under the list's ⋯ menu Export (of what the widget shows), Import (CSV and XLSX) and, for an administrator of a table that posts into a grid, Sync to grid — each offered only when the server allows it to that person. The new-record button, named after the table's record (New account, New deal), is shown only when the widget's developer added it (the widget's Show the + New record button), and opens the form in a dialog. A shown widget title heads the list, with the filters the widget keeps under it. The developer screen has no record controls.
  • Values are checked on the server. A dimension field takes a leaf member the person can see — a hidden member is answered like an unknown code; a metric field chosen by name takes an input metric the person can see; required fields must be filled; numbers, dates and true/false are checked. An import checks every row the same way and imports nothing if one fails; the rows are then written together, so a file that stops part-way (a lost connection) leaves none of its records, and they post into the grid as one batch with one recalculation.
  • Access rules decide where a record may post. A save that would post into a cell the person may not write — a member or metric that is read-only for them, a workflow-locked cell — is refused with a message naming the member or metric (a hidden one is not named). Where a field posts on save, the member picker leaves out the members the person may only read. A draft that does not post yet may name such a member: an approver who may write there approves it.
  • What people see follows their access rules. A record naming a member or a metric hidden from a person — through a dimension field, a fixed metric field, or a metric field chosen by name — is left out of their list, search, totals and export.
  • Posting to cells is a separate object. A table mapping (Integrations › Records) decides which field feeds which input metric, at which dimension members, for which record statuses. Metric and Dimension field types exist so the mapping has something to read.
  • Where tables appear. Users and business admins open a table through a dashboard Records widget. A table that is on no dashboard is not reachable by them: place every table people fill in on a dashboard their business role is granted. The chat connector and the AI Developer read a table's records too (read-only; query_records).

Grids

Sidebar: Developer › Grids

A grid is a selection of metrics and dimensions that is shown as one table. It holds no data of its own; the data lives in the model. The same metric can only sit in one grid, so grids partition the model's metrics into the tables users will edit.

Rules and behaviour

  • One grid per metric. Ticking a metric that already belongs to another grid is refused by the server and the box stays unticked after refresh. Untick it in the other grid first. Dimensions can be shared freely.
  • Same revision. Metrics and grid must be in the selected revision; a mismatch is refused.
  • Deleting a grid deletes its widgets. Every dashboard widget that showed the grid disappears; the dashboards themselves stay.
  • Rename and tags. The pencil renames a grid and edits its tags; widgets that show the grid keep showing it. Tags travel with the grid: a new revision and a model export copy them.
  • Where the table comes from. A grid becomes visible to users through a dashboard Grid widget (Dashboards chapter). The widget decides which dimension is rows, columns or a selector; the grid only decides the ingredients.
  • Level display. Choosing Leaves only hides parent members from the axis; totals for parents are still computed and appear where the widget shows them.
