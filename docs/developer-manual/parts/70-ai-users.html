AI Developer

Sidebar: Developer › AI Developer

A chat assistant that can read the model and, when you approve, build, change and remove what you build on the other Developer screens, under the same checks. Model changes happen in an isolated AI draft revision that you promote or discard, so the live model is never touched by accident. Three kinds of change are live as soon as you execute the plan, as they are on their own screens: business roles, user access rules, and form records posted through a form integration.

Activity panelThis session's proposal history.
ProposalsEvery plan is tried against the model before you see it; a plan that would fail goes back to the assistant to correct, so what you confirm is expected to run. It may correct a plan three times, and more (up to eight) while each attempt fails fewer steps; then it proposes the steps that pass and the chat lists the ones the check left out, and after that it stops and says what fails — it is told never to end a turn asking whether to propose. A plan that runs but has a formula that looks like a typical mistake — a Percentage metric multiplied in without dividing by 100, or SUMIFS(src, Region, Region) on a cell that carries every dimension of src (it is the cell's own value; the total is a LOOKUP of the other dimension's total member) — goes back once with a warning; proposed again unchanged, it is shown with the warning above it ("The plan check warns: …"). If a step still fails when confirmed, the steps after it are not run (they usually depend on it), and the summary says so.
Converted filesSpreadsheets the assistant converted to the import layout, each with CSV and Excel download buttons. Nothing in them has been imported.
Draft banner"Working in an isolated draft revision — changes here won't affect the live model until promoted." Promote to Active makes the draft the active revision and finishes the session: the message box closes with "This session's draft was promoted…" and a New session button — a new session works on the promoted revision; Discard asks Discard this draft? and permanently removes every model change the AI made in this session (business roles, access rules and posted form records are not in the draft and stay).

What it can do

  • Read tools: describe metrics, dimensions, grids (with display levels), dashboard folders, dashboards and each widget, revisions, workflows, triggers, forms, form integrations, business roles and users; validate formulas and workflows; answer questions about the model and attached documents.
  • Write tools, always through an approved plan:
    • metrics: create, update, delete;
    • dimensions: create, change (update_dimension: name, rollup rule, parent dimension, property grouping, deriving its members) and delete; members: add, change (code, label, parent, period dates, properties), delete, and generate a run of periods; typed member properties: declare, rename, retype, delete;
    • grids: create, rename, delete, add and remove metrics and dimensions, set a dimension's display level;
    • dashboards: folders, dashboards and widgets — create, change (a widget's place, size, title, what it shows) and delete;
    • tags, revisions, and user access rules (a business-admin capability given to the assistant on purpose);
    • workflows, triggers, business roles, forms and form integrations (below);
    • data: import an attached spreadsheet into a grid (metric values) or a dimension (members), first as a dry run that names every row that would fail, then all-or-nothing into the draft; save the mapping as a re-runnable Excel/CSV integration; create, change and delete data exports (see Integrations › Data Export). Exports created in a session are listed above the message box with a Download button.
    Each change is the screen's own action with the screen's checks: a dimension or member a formula still uses is refused, a new member code carries its stored values over, a leaf that gains its first child hands its values down to it.
  • The tenant's plan limits apply to the assistant as they apply to you.
  • Plans are capped at 100 steps; long tasks are continued in a follow-up plan.
  • What stays yours: publishing and test-running a workflow, promoting or discarding the draft and deleting revisions, who is in a business role, users, configuring data connectors (REST API and Google Sheets), typing business values and form records (the assistant imports a spreadsheet you attach to its chat, and writes single input values such as a setting with write_input_values, into its draft), and creating applications. Database tables are migrated automatically; there is nothing to run.
Workflows and forms. The assistant builds the whole chain a workflow needs: the business roles its steps are assigned to, the definition with every step type the editor offers, the automation rule that starts it, and the form and form integration that feed it. It also archives, restores and duplicates workflows, renames and deletes roles and sets the dashboards they may open, and backfills a form integration. It runs the same Validate check as the Workflows editor and reports the verdict with every change. Two things stay yours: publishing a workflow (the assistant leaves it as a draft and tells you so) and adding members to a role. The assistant uses the same validation as the screens, so a formula or a workflow it proposes is rejected under the same rules you would be.

Users

Roles

Sidebar: Developer › Roles (hidden when you also hold business_admin: the Business Admin console's Roles tab is the same screen with members)

Named business roles ("Budget Approvers", "Regional Managers"). Workflow tasks and approvals are assigned to these roles by name, or to the platform roles. A developer creates the roles their workflows need and decides which dashboards each may open; who is in a role is decided by a business admin.

ControlWhat it does
New role name… / Add roleCreates a role in the application's workspace.
RenameRenames the role. Workflow steps name roles by name: update the steps that named the old one.
ConfigureOpens Visible Dashboards: tick the dashboards of the revision you are working on that the role's members may open, then Save dashboard access. Grants on dashboards of other revisions are kept.
Delete (bin)Confirm Delete role?: removes the role, its members and its dashboard grants.

Where the rest of access is configured

Three things decide what a user sees and may change. Role membership and access rules live in the Business Admin console (sidebar group Business Admin), which a developer sees only when they also hold the business_admin role or ask a business admin:

  • Roles. The roles above, with their members.
  • Dashboard access. Per role, the dashboards its members may open (Save dashboard access). A user who holds any business role sees only the dashboards granted to their roles; one who holds none sees every dashboard.
  • Access rules. Per user, an Access level of write, read or hidden for individual dimension members and metrics (Save rules). Hidden members disappear from grids, charts and selectors; read makes cells read-only; rules on a parent member cascade to its descendants.
