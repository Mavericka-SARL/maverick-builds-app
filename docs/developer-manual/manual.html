About this manual

This manual describes everything a person with the developer role can do in the maverickbuilds.app console, and the concepts needed to use those screens well. Part I explains the platform and its rules. Part II is a reference, one chapter per sidebar item, in sidebar order: what the screen is for, a screenshot, every control, and the rules behind it. Part III lists limits, common problems and terms.

Everything here was taken from the console as built on the date on the cover, and every screenshot is from a live session. Exact labels are printed in bold; values you type are in monospace. Nothing in this manual requires the API or a script.

The platform and the developer role

What maverickbuilds.app is

maverickbuilds.app is a platform for building governed business applications without code. An application combines three kinds of capability that a developer configures and business users operate:

ModeWhat the developer buildsWhat business users do
PlanningDimensions, metrics and formulas, grids, dashboards, revisionsType and import values, read charts and KPIs, plan in grids
CRUDForms, form-to-metric posting, imports, integrationsSubmit and manage records that feed the plan
ExecutionWorkflows, triggers, notificationsStart approvals, act on tasks in their inbox, get notified

The console is one application with one sidebar. Roles are additive: each role adds groups to the sidebar, never a second console. A developer sees the Build group (Models, Metrics, Dimensions, Forms, Grids, Dashboards, Workflows, Triggers, Integrations, Dependency Graph, AI Developer) and the Govern group (Users). A developer who also holds business_admin sees the business Plan and Admin groups as well; a tenant or platform administrator sees the administration groups.

The five roles

A developer can grant business_admin and business_user, never developer or administrator roles. A developer works inside the tenants they have been given; objects in other tenants are invisible.

Core concepts

Ownership: tenant, application, model, revision

Tenant (customer)
  └ Application            mode planning / CRUD / execution; what users open
      └ Model              container of definitions and data; one is the "business default"
          └ Revision       the unit of change; one revision is ACTIVE (live)
              ├ dimensions, members, properties
              ├ metrics, formulas, dependency graph
              ├ grids, dashboards, folders, widgets
              ├ forms, form mappings, integrations, imports
              ├ workflows, automation rules
              └ facts (the values)
  • Tenant. The isolation boundary. Users, applications and data belong to exactly one tenant.
  • Application. What users open. Created by an administrator. Has a mode and a status.
  • Model. Owns everything below. An application usually has one; when it has several, the business default model is the one business consoles open.
  • Revision. Every definition and every value belongs to a revision. Exactly one revision per model is active: that is what business users see and edit. A developer selects a revision on the Models screen and every Build screen then works inside it.

Revisions

  • Creating a revision copies the model. A new revision is a deep copy of the current one: metrics and their dependencies, dimensions with members and properties, grids, dashboards with widgets and folders, forms with their records and mappings, integrations, workflows and automation rules, and the latest value of every cell. Internal references are remapped so the copy is self-contained.
  • Activating switches the live revision; it can be changed again. The active revision cannot be deleted.
  • Build in a non-active revision, then activate. Users are not affected until you do. Workflow instances that were running keep the definition they started with.
  • System-managed revisions are written only by workflow approvals (approved scopes are copied into them) and are read-only for typing and imports.
  • Export and import. Tenant administrators can export a revision as a package, with or without data, and import it into another tenant or environment. Developers do not have this control.
  • There is no separate "version" concept. The revision is the unit of change; do not look for one.

Dimensions and members

  • A dimension is an axis; members are its values, identified by a code and shown by a label. Members can have parents, forming a hierarchy; parents are totals of their children under the dimension's rollup rule.
  • A dimension can be declared a child of another dimension (for example Cost Centre under Department); its members are then attached to members of the parent dimension.
  • Properties are typed attributes on members (text, number, date) that can drive groupings and rollups.

Metrics, formulas and calculation

  • Input metrics hold typed, imported or posted values. Calculated metrics are defined by a formula over other metrics and are never typed into.
  • Calculation is server-side and immediate. Writing a cell, changing a formula, deleting a metric or member, posting a form record and committing an import all recalculate every dependent metric before the screen refreshes. There is no "recalculate" button.
  • Dependency graph. The server derives which metrics depend on which from the formulas, orders them, refuses cycles and self-references, and shows the result in the Metrics "Used by" column and the Dependency Graph screen.
  • Aggregation rules decide totals: Sum, Average, Count, Rate (numerator ÷ denominator), Formula (evaluate the formula on the aggregated inputs, for ratios and percentages).
  • A missing input leaves the calculated cell blank at that intersection; totals are computed over the intersections that exist.

Formula language

=revenue - cogs
=IF(revenue = 0, 0, (revenue - cogs) / revenue)
=ROUND(quantity * unit_price, 2)
={salary} * 0.25
  • References are metric technical names, case-insensitive; the legacy {name} form is accepted. The leading = is optional.
  • Literals: numbers, quoted strings, TRUE, FALSE. Operators: + - * / ^, = <> < <= > >=, &, unary minus, parentheses. Arguments may be separated by commas or semicolons.
CategoryFunctions
LogicIF, IFS, AND, OR, NOT, IFERROR, IFNA, SWITCH
MathABS, INT, ROUND, ROUNDUP, ROUNDDOWN, CEILING, FLOOR, MOD, POWER, SQRT
Aggregate over argumentsSUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX, COUNT, COUNTA
TextCONCAT, TEXTJOIN, LEN, LEFT, RIGHT, MID, UPPER, LOWER, TRIM, TEXT, SUBSTITUTE
DateTODAY, DATE, YEAR, MONTH, DAY, DAYS, EDATE, EOMONTH
Time seriesPREVIOUS, NEXT, LAG, LEAD, OFFSET, MOVINGSUM, CUMULATE, DECUMULATE, MONTHTODATE, QUARTERTODATE, YEARTODATE — only on a metric whose grid carries a Time dimension (see Dimensions); they move along its leaf periods, and an aggregate period (H1, FY26) shows its leaves reduced by the metric's Time summary. LAG(revenue, 1, 0) reads the previous period (0 before the first); MOVINGSUM(sales, -2, 0, AVERAGE) is a trailing three-period average; CUMULATE(sales) runs from the first period. Offsets are whole-number literals. An opening/closing balance pair — opening = LAG(closing, 1, 100), closing = opening + flow — is allowed because time breaks the cycle; any other cycle is refused.

Not available: spreadsheet cell addresses (A1), conditional aggregates (SUMIF, SUMIFS, AVERAGEIFS and similar), lookups (XLOOKUP, INDEX, MATCH), dotted property references (employee.region), parent or children functions, the later Anaplan time functions (POST, SPREAD, TIMESUM, YEARVALUE and similar), and user-written code. Cross-dimension and property rollups are configured on grids and dimensions, not written as formulas. Validation happens when you save a metric: unknown names, unsupported functions, syntax errors, self-references and cycles are refused.

Data

  • Values are append-only. Every write adds a new fact; the latest one at an intersection is the value. Nothing is ever overwritten in place, which is what makes history and audit possible.
  • Formats (Number, Percentage, Currency with symbol and decimals, Boolean, Text) control display everywhere the metric appears.
  • Totals in grids, charts and KPIs are computed by the server from the aggregation rules, never by the browser.
  • Imports run in Incremental mode (add or update the listed cells), Sync / replace mode (the file is authoritative for the cells it lists) or Full reload (delete all data of the revision first).

Grids, dashboards and what users see

  • A grid is a selection of metrics and dimensions. A metric belongs to one grid at most.
  • A dashboard is a canvas of widgets. A grid widget decides which dimension is rows, columns or a selector; charts and KPIs read from grids and metrics; form, trigger, integration, text and import widgets complete the page.
  • Business users reach a dashboard only when a business role they belong to has been granted it. Roles, grants and per-user access rules are managed in the Business Admin console.

Visibility and locks

  • Access rules per user and per dimension member or metric: write (default), read (visible, not editable) or hidden (removed from grids, charts, selectors and totals shown to that user). A rule on a parent member cascades to its descendants. Rules are applied on the server; hidden data never reaches the browser.
  • Workflow locks. While a workflow instance about a grid, metric or member scope is in progress or approved, that scope is locked: typing and imports are refused with "… is locked by an in-progress or approved workflow".
  • Calculated metrics are always read-only. System-managed revisions are read-only for everyone.

Workflows, triggers and notifications

  • A workflow definition is steps and routes; a trigger (automation rule) starts it on an event; an instance is one run, keeping the definition it started with; a task is one step waiting for a person.
  • Tasks appear in the business Workflow Inbox of everyone holding the assigned role. Business users submit, business admins decide.
  • Notifications are in-app (the bell) and are produced by notification steps, rework and failed starts.

Audit

Every change made through the console, by developers and business users alike, is recorded in the audit log with the acting user, the object and the action. Administrators read it on the Audit Log screen; developers have no screen for it, but should know that their edits are attributable.

Dimensions

Sidebar: Build › Dimensions

A dimension is an axis of the model (Department, Region, Product, Period). Its members are the values on that axis, optionally arranged in a hierarchy, and each member can carry property values used for grouping and rollups. A dimension is either Standard or Time — chosen explicitly when it is created and fixed afterwards. Only a Time dimension gives formulas a calendar to move along (PREVIOUS, LAG, MOVINGSUM, …); naming a standard dimension "month" does not.

Rules and behaviour

  • Codes are identity. Data, imports and formulas refer to members by code; labels are for display. Changing a code re-keys the member for future writes.
  • Hierarchy and totals. A parent's value is the rollup of its children using the dimension's rollup rule (sum by default). Grids can show all levels, one level, or leaves only (Grids › Show).
  • Deleting a member deletes its descendants and triggers recalculation of every metric that had values there.
  • Properties are the basis of property-derived groupings (for example grouping cost centres by their region property). Editing property values per member is available here; building derived dimensions from properties is only partly exposed in the console.
  • Dimensions are per revision, like everything else under Build.

Rules and behaviour

  • Forms are per revision. The list and the create action use the selected revision. Editing never moves a form to another revision.
  • Same name overwrites. Creating a form whose technical name already exists in the revision silently replaces that form's label and fields. Pick a new name for a new form.
  • Delete cascades. Deleting a form removes every dashboard widget that shows it and every record ever submitted to it.
  • Records live in the business console. Business users create, view and export records there (CSV and XLSX). The developer screen has no record controls.
  • Posting to cells is a separate object. A form mapping (Integrations › Form Records) decides which field feeds which input metric, at which dimension members, for which record statuses. Metric and Dimension field types exist so the mapping has something to read.
  • Where forms appear. Business users open a form through a dashboard Form widget. A form that is on no dashboard is not reachable by them.

Grids

Rules and behaviour

  • One grid per metric. Ticking a metric that already belongs to another grid is refused by the server and the box stays unticked after refresh. Untick it in the other grid first. Dimensions can be shared freely.
  • Same revision. Metrics and grid must be in the selected revision; a mismatch is refused.
  • Deleting a grid deletes its widgets. Every dashboard widget that showed the grid disappears; the dashboards themselves stay.
  • No rename. The screen has no rename control; create a new grid if the name must change.
  • Where the table comes from. A grid becomes visible to users through a dashboard Grid widget (Dashboards chapter). The widget decides which dimension is rows, columns or a selector; the grid only decides the ingredients.
  • Level display. Choosing Leaves only hides parent members from the axis; totals for parents are still computed and appear where the widget shows them.

The canvas: Design and Preview

The toolbar shows Dashboards (back), the dashboard name, the widget count, the Design / Preview switch and, in Design mode, the widget palette: Grid, Chart, Form, Metric KPI, Trigger, Integration, Text, Image, Import.

Text takes a small Markdown subset — # headings, **bold**, *italic*, `code`, bullet and numbered lists, > quotes, --- rules and [label](https://example.com) links. Plain text that uses none of it is shown as typed. Image takes a PNG, JPEG, SVG, WebP or GIF of up to 512 KB, chosen with a file picker; the picture is stored with the dashboard, so it travels with a revision copy, an export and an import, and it takes alternative text for screen readers and a Fit of "show all of it" or "fill the box". Together they are what an explanatory page is built from — the tour a new workspace starts with is written in exactly these widgets.

  • Design is for placing and configuring widgets. Nothing is live.
  • Preview renders the dashboard exactly as a business user gets it, against the revision you are designing, and includes property edits you have not saved yet. Values typed into a grid in Preview are real writes.

Moving, sizing, selecting

  • Drag the widget header to move; drag the right edge, bottom edge or corner to resize. Everything snaps to 20 px.
  • Keyboard: arrow keys nudge by 20 px, Shift+arrow by 100 px, Alt+arrow resizes, Escape deselects.
  • Widgets may not overlap. A colliding drop snaps back and the other widget shows "Overlaps another widget" while you drag.
  • Minimum sizes: grid 600×300, chart 360×240, form 360×300, KPI 220×120, import 320×280, text 240×100, buttons 40×20.
  • The × on a card asks Delete widget?; the source object (grid, form, rule) is not deleted.

What business users get: the planning grid

  • Editing. Input metrics are inputs; a value is committed on Enter or when leaving the cell. Non-numeric text is discarded. "Saving…" and "Save failed: …" appear while writing.
  • Read-only cells. Calculated metrics, aggregated parent members, members or metrics the user's access rules mark read-only, and grids that mirror another grid are not editable.
  • Selectors. One selector per context dimension the widget owns, plus a Metric selector when Metrics is in the context zone. Selectors are searchable trees; with synced widgets, a choice applies to all of them.
  • Click to select. Clicking a row or column label sets that member in every synced widget. The legend says so when sync is available.
  • Totals. A hierarchical column axis adds a read-only Total column per parent group; a flat row axis with more than one row adds a bold Total footer row.
  • Hierarchies render fully expanded, indented per level, parents in bold. There is no collapse control.
  • Grid actions (⋯): Export the grid's values as a file, and Pivot data, a panel where the user drags Metrics and dimensions between Rows, Columns and Context selectors.
  • Hidden members never reach the browser: the server strips members and metrics the user may not see and flags read-only ones. If a widget is pinned to a member the viewer cannot see, the first visible member is used instead.

Making a dashboard reachable

A business user sees a dashboard only when one of their business roles has been granted it. Grants are managed on the Users screen (Users chapter). Until then the dashboard exists only for developers.

Steps

Add a step with the Add step palette (Task, Approval, Condition, Notification, Join); it is inserted after the selected step. Rows in the step list can be moved up or down, duplicated and deleted. The canvas is a picture of the list: click a card to select it; there is no dragging or edge drawing on the canvas.

Step typeProperties
AllStep name (required), Instructions shown to the person acting.
TaskAssignee roles (platform roles business_admin and business_user, plus named roles from the Business Admin console; a hint says where to create one), SLA hours, Require comment, Completion button label (default "Complete"), Next step or Parallel branches (add with + parallel).
ApprovalApprover roles, SLA hours, Require comment, On approve →, On reject →.
ConditionContext field, Operator (equals, not equals, greater than, ≥, less than, ≤, contains, is empty, is not empty), Value, If true →, If false →. Evaluated automatically; if the value is missing the inbox asks a person to pick the branch.

JoinWaits for parallel branches, then Next step.

Route targets are any other step, or End: Completed, End: Rejected, End: Cancelled. Routing to an earlier step is how rework loops are built; there is no separate rework control. A step with no route falls through to the next step in list order, so the order of the list matters.

What Validate checks

  • Name present, at least one step, every step named and typed.
  • Tasks and approvals have a role; approvals have both routes; conditions have a condition and both routes; a notification to a role names the role.
  • Routes point at existing steps; every step is reachable from the first; some path reaches an end.
  • A loop is allowed only if a task or approval sits on it ("nobody could ever stop it" otherwise).

What business users experience

  • Starting. Manual workflows appear under "Start a workflow" in the business Workflow Inbox and behind dashboard Trigger buttons. Context variables become the start form; Dimension member and Metric variables are multi-selects, one instance per combination, at most 20 per submission.
  • Acting. Tasks show the step name, workflow, requester, due date (SLA), instructions, a context summary, a comment box (required when the step says so) and the buttons: Approve / Reject, the task's completion label, or Continue as true / Continue as false for an unevaluable condition. Business admins decide; business users submit and also act on tasks assigned to their role.
  • Locking. While an instance is in progress or approved, the cells in its scope are locked against edits and imports; the inbox says "the scoped data is locked until a decision is made".
  • Rework. A route back to an earlier step re-activates it with a "Sent back for rework (n)" note and notifies the assignee. At most 20 reworks per instance.
  • Notifications are in-app: the bell in every console. Notification steps, rework, and failed starts produce them.

Rules and behaviour

  • A rule is event → workflow, nothing more. Conditions and actions belong to the workflow's steps. Time schedules exist for integrations, not for rules.
  • Dashboard buttons can only run rules that are manual and enabled. A business user sees a disabled button with the reason when that stops being true.
  • A failed start (for example a duplicate refused by "one active instance per scope") produces a "Workflow could not start" notification for the person who triggered it.
  • Rules are per revision and are copied with it.

Where the rest of access is configured

Three things decide what a business user sees and may change. All three live in the Business Admin console (sidebar group Admin), which a developer sees only when they also hold the business_admin role or ask a business admin:

  • Roles. Named business roles ("Budget Approvers", "Regional Managers") with members. Workflow tasks and approvals are assigned to these roles or to the platform roles.
  • Dashboard access. Per role, tick the dashboards its members may open (Save dashboard access). A dashboard on no role is invisible to business users.
  • Access rules. Per user, an Access level of write, read or hidden for individual dimension members and metrics (Save rules). Hidden members disappear from grids, charts and selectors; read makes cells read-only; rules on a parent member cascade to its descendants.
